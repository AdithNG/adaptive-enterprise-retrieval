Hi Jana,

Thanks for the clear constraints and the diagram — helpful. High-level summary first, then a recommended phased plan.

Quick tradeoffs (short):
- VPC Peering:
  * Pros: Lowest latency intra-region/peer-account; straightforward routing; no per-flow NAT by default.
  * Cons: No cross-region peering without inter-account Transit solutions; scales poorly as number of accounts/regions grows (mesh complexity); does not expose single stable egress IP for external services.

- PrivateLink (interface endpoints):
  * Pros: Endpoint sits in your VPC subnets; stable endpoint ENIs that can be fronted by NLB or used with AWS endpoint policies; reduces blast radius (service owner exposes only the interface).
  * Cons: Per-region setup, per-AZ ENIs, cost per hour/GB; not ideal for very high sustained throughput unless fronted by dedicated NLB or regional load-balancing; cross-account access is supported but you still need endpoint configuration per region.

- Site-to-site (S2S) VPN with BGP (from Equinix):
  * Pros: Familiar operational model for your team; gives you control-plane and data-plane via routable prefixes; can provide stable egress IPs via NAT at your edge; easy to put behind your firewall/IDS.
  * Cons: IPsec MTU issues, variable throughput on managed VPN, need HA tunnels per region and careful BGP path preference to avoid asymmetric routing. For large sustained throughput, youll likely look at Direct Connect or colocation + accelerated VPN appliances.

Recommendation (opinionated, staged):
1) Control plane over PrivateLink (pilot in us-east-1):
   - Expose Redwood control/management endpoints via PrivateLink in each region. This gives you a stable, least-privileged control channel and avoids touching your public egress. PrivateLink keeps the control plane traffic entirely in the region and supports endpoint policies for RBAC.
2) Data plane over BGP-enabled S2S (pilot to start in us-east-1):
   - Use your existing Equinix routers to terminate BGP sessions into the VPC (via VGW/Transit Gateway + Direct Connect if bandwidth requires). This gives you predictable routing and the ability to NAT at your colocation to present immutable egress addresses.
3) Use Transit Gateway (or cloud carrier interconnect) as the regional backbone if you need cross-account/cross-VPC scale:
   - For multi-region scaling (eu-west-1), prefer TGW + Transit Gateway Inter-Region Peering or a central hub to avoid mesh peering. This keeps route tables manageable and lets you centralize inspection/NAT if desired.
4) Fallback and HA:
   - PrivateLink for the control plane acts as a fallback if a VPN path degrades; you can configure health checks and failover to another region. For data-plane HA, plan active/active BGP with local preference tuning and a well-defined path-preference matrix.

Gotchas weve seen:
- MTU/path MTU discovery failures when IPsec + VXLAN are layered; test with your packet sizes (we push large payloads frequently).
- Asymmetric routing when you mix TGW and direct peering: keep source/destination checks and return path determinism in mind.
- PrivateLink throughput: many teams underestimate per-ENI bandwidth limits if youre doing large concurrent streams; front it with NLB and ensure cross-AZ capacity.
- Auditing: ensure flow logs and VPC ENI-level logging are enabled so you can produce forensics for compliance.

Ben / Marcus,

Adding operational considerations and a suggested pilot checklist:

Operational bullets:
- IPAM and route ownership: decide whether EdgeBank owns the on-prem CID space advertised into the cloud or if we allocate a separate overlay. Avoid overlapping CID ranges.
- Observability: require VPC Flow Logs at the subnet ENI level + VPN/NVA telemetry shipped to your SIEM. Include packet captures during pilot windows.
- Throttling & rate limits: test sustained concurrency against model endpoints; if we see request-level throttles, well need to introduce sticky sessions or connection pooling at the NLB/NVA layer.
- Egress NAT: to provide immutable egress IPs, place NAT64/NAT64-equivalent at the colocation (recommended) and advertise those IPs via BGP to cloud region.
- Security: mutual TLS for service-to-service control plane, and mTLS + client certs on PrivateLink if you want defence-in-depth.

Pilot checklist (minimum viable):
1) Setup PrivateLink interface endpoint in us-east-1 and grant access to EdgeBank AWS account (endpoint acceptance flow).
2) Configure BGP session from Equinix router to a TGW/VGW in us-east-1; advertise EdgeBank prefixes.
3) Run perf tests: 1h steady-state at 50% expected throughput, 30m spike tests with max payloads, and MTU flood test.
4) Observability baseline: enable flow logs and sampling pcap, demo dashboard for compliance report.
5) Failover test: shut VPN1 down and validate control plane over PrivateLink +/- failover routing.
