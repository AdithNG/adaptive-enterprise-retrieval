Team / Jordan — I reviewed ACME_MSA_redline_v3.docx. High-level legal position below plus draft language suggestions. Attaching Redwood_response_redline_v1.docx with inline edits (key clauses summarized here).

Legal assessment:
- Regulatory fines (GDPR/CCPA/PCI): We generally resist unlimited liability, but will consider a carve-out for regulator-imposed fines where Redwood's breach of its obligations directly causes the fine. Any indemnity must be subject to: (i) Redwood's right to cure where feasible; (ii) prompt notice and control of defense; (iii) mitigation obligations; and (iv) a requirement that fines be final and non-appealable before payment (or subject to escrow/interim arrangements).
- Third-party penalty claims (payment networks, PCI assessors): We can accept responsibility for penalties directly attributable to Redwood's failure to follow Redwood-managed payment processing obligations, limited to actual fines imposed and excluding consequential/statutory multiplier damages.
- Subrogation/assignment: We do not object to reasonable insurer subrogation, but will resist broad assignment rights that allow a customer to transfer claims to third parties without Redwood's consent. We recommend a narrowly tailored clause allowing assignment/subrogation by the customer's insurer with notice to Redwood and cooperation rights.

Suggested compromise language (high-level):
"Notwithstanding the limitation of liability set forth herein, (i) Redwood shall be liable for and shall indemnify Customer for regulatory fines and penalties to the extent such fines are the direct result of Redwood's material breach of its obligations under the Data Processing Addendum or willful misconduct, provided Customer notifies Redwood promptly, allows Redwood to cure, and the amount is finally determined by a binding governmental order; and (ii) Customer's insurer may pursue subrogation against Redwood for such fines, provided Customer provides Redwood notice and the insurer does not materially expand the scope of the claim beyond the underlying government determination."

Risk notes: we should avoid open-ended indemnities. If Acme insists on unlimited exposure, consider requiring increased insurance limits (e.g., cyber liability + regulatory fines endorsement) or a monetary backstop (cap tied to fees or a fixed $ amount).
