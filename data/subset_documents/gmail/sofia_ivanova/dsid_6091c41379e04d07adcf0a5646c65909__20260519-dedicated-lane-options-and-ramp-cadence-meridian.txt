Hi Elaine, Sofia — quick technical clarification and a concrete proposal for Dr. Patel's warmup question.

Geo-fallback lane model
- We can provide geo-fallback as reserved hot-standby replicas (counted against committed capacity) or as on-demand burst nodes. For predictable failover and SLA-backed RTOs I recommend reserving a small standby fleet (4–6 GPUs) in a secondary region — this keeps failover time <120s. If you prefer not to reserve, on-demand nodes are possible but add variability in failover and cost.

Warmup and token-hour estimate (rough)
- Baseline Guard (16 GPUs): full prefix/KV warmup ~8 GPU-hours (prefill small KV sets) -> estimated 12,000 token-hours of work at an average token-rate of 1.5k tokens/min per GPU during warmup.
- Anchored SLA (32 GPUs): incremental warmup per wave ~18 GPU-hours -> estimated 45,000 token-hours.
- Burst-Ready (48 GPUs): cold-to-hot activation ~36 GPU-hours; if we prefill caches ahead of traffic, we reduce effective warmup to ~12 GPU-hours but that requires reserved burst windows + scheduled prefill calls.

Quantization tradeoff (cost impact)
- Using an 8-bit profile for non-critical batch scoring reduces GPU-dwell by ~20–30% on average. That can drop the effective committed GPU count for Baseline by 2–3 GPUs. We will run a short bench for your model in our lab (need sample model or representative layer sizes) and provide a precise delta.

Procurement flexibility
- We can include a 60-day ramp-down clause for reserved capacity with a phased decommit schedule (30% -> 15% -> 0% across 60 days) and apply a credit schedule for unused committed hours. I'll loop Sean for commercial confirmation.
