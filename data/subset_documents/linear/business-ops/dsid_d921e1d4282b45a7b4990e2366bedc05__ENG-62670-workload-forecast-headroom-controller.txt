Workload-forecast-based headroom controller for Dedicated capacity

Introduce a headroom controller that uses short-horizon workload forecasts to proactively provision and maintain throughput guarantees for Dedicated capacity. The controller combines forecasting, conservative step sizing, and circuit-breaker safety limits to reduce SLA violations and avoid oscillatory scale behavior during traffic spikes and diurnal patterns.
Current autoscaler reacts to observed metrics (p95 latency, tokens/sec) and applies conservative steps with cooldowns. We observe two issues: (1) transient spikes cause reactive overprovisioning or missed SLOs before scaling completes, and (2) cost-inefficient oscillations when traffic rapidly changes. Several related tickets implemented safety envelopes and conservative stepping, but none incorporate predictive headroom. Customer complaints from three enterprise Dedicated tenants noted latency regressions during predictable marketing spikes.
Build a lightweight forecasting module (exponential smoothing + seasonality detector) in the autoscaler service to predict 1-30min horizon throughput and tail latency proxies. Compute a headroom target: predicted_peak * safety_margin + min_capacity. Use an adaptive controller that translates headroom delta into scale actions with a PI-inspired controller, but capped by per-tenant hard limits and global fleet safety windows. Integrate a circuit-breaker that prevents scaling above emergency ceiling unless sustained over 2 minutes. Provide knobs: forecast_horizon, safety_margin_pct, max_step_percent, cooldown_s, and emergency_ceiling. Expose metrics and a dashboard and add canary rollout to 5% of Dedicated clusters.
Use single-model ETS (error-trend-seasonality) style forecasting implemented in Go for low latency and deterministic resource use
Forecast horizons: default 5 minutes; configurable up to 30 minutes per project
Controller algorithm: PI-inspired mapping of headroom delta to steps with exponential backoff on repeated failures
Safety limits: per-tenant floor/ceiling plus a cross-tenant emergency ceiling enforced by fleet controller
Circuit-breaker triggers require predicted headroom > emergency_ceiling OR observed sustained p95 breach for >120s
No immediate autoscale-down on short negative forecast spikes: require sustained 3x check intervals before reduction
Forecasting reduces missed SLOs for predictable patterns but introduces risk if model overestimates — mitigated by hard emergency ceilings
Adding forecasting increases autoscaler complexity and CPU usage; we chose simple ETS to minimize compute and maintenance burden
Longer forecast horizons improve anticipation but increase false positives; default is conservative (5m) and configurable
PI-style controller is easier to reason about than full RL or optimization but may be suboptimal for complex multi-tenant interactions
Canary rollout and strict safety limits increase deployment time but are necessary to prevent fleet-wide regressions
