Adaptive burst handover and regional SLA safeguard

Problem: During high-frequency client bursts (sudden traffic spikes from mobile apps and partner integrations) we observe two correlated failures: 1) regional quota controllers lose short-term state during control-plane blips causing asymmetric quota attribution and client unfairness; 2) downstream circuit-breaker tripping cascades unevenly across regions causing competent regions to be overloaded as traffic flaps to them. Impact is elevated p95/p99 latency and transient errors that violate SLO windows.

Goal: Implement an adaptive burst handover protocol with explicit handoff tokens, short-lived lease reconciliation, and SLA-aware region admission gating so that when a region is degraded or under limiter outage we can: (a) preserve proportional fairness across tenants, (b) avoid thundering herd to healthy regions, and (c) meet regional SLO commitments with graceful degraded responses.

Proposal summary:
- Introduce transient "handover token" issued by regional admission proxy on lease steal events. Tokens carry minimal metadata (tenant, sequence, granted-burst-units, expiry) and are cryptographically signed for short lived trust across regions.
- Implement lease-reconciliation worker that consumes tokens and applies idempotent credit application to receiving-region quota stores with TTL-based reverse compensations.
- Add SLA-aware admission gating: a policy layer that monitors regional SLO budget (error budget and p95 latency) and decides whether to accept handover tokens or respond with a calibrated degraded response (rate-limited 503 or 202 with retry-after).
- Circuit-breaker coordination: extend circuit state propagation to include a second-class flag (accept-handovers) so that circuit-open in a region both rejects new local admission and signals callers to try alternative regions in a staggered, rate-limited way rather than immediate fanout.
- Observability: new metrics (handover_issued, handover_accepted, handover_rejected_by_sla, handover_compensations_applied, handover_replay_failures). Dashboard and SLO alerts for cross-region imbalance and compensation deltas.

Why this differs from existing work: Many previous tickets focus on local admission or centralized quota reservoirs; this work focuses on safe, auditable cross-region handovers and SLA-aware acceptance logic to avoid overload-growth in healthy regions while preserving fairness during partial control-plane outages.

Risks and trade-offs:
- Complexity: cryptographic tokens and cross-region reconciliation add code surface and latency for handovers. Mitigation: tokens are optional and only used during detected degraded conditions; normal path unchanged.
- Eventual consistency: compensations might be delayed; SLO windows must tolerate small compensation delays. Mitigation: choose token expiry and lease TTL conservatively and surface compensation deltas to SLO monitors.
- Security: tokens traverse regions; need signature verification and replay protection. Mitigation: short TTL, nonces, and KMS-signed keys rotated monthly.

Rollout plan (high level):
1) Implement token issuance and local test harness (unit/integration).
2) Canary acceptance logic in a single non-critical region (us-west-2) with simulation traffic.
3) Enable cross-region reconciliation and staged canary to eu-west-1, then ap-southeast-1.
4) Full rollout with policy default: accept handovers only when target region has >= 30% of error budget and p95 < configured threshold.

Monitoring and runbook:
- Dashboards: per-tenant compensation delta, handover acceptance rate, regional SLO remaining.
- Alerts: >5% of traffic compensated for >90s, >10% handover_replay_failures in 5m, and regional SLO margin <5%.
- Runbook actions: throttle handover issuance, enable static pinning for high-value tenants, evict non-critical tenants from handover acceptance, revert to local degraded responses.
