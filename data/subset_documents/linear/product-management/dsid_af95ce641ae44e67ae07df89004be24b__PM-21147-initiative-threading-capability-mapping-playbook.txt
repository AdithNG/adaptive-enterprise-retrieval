Initiative threading and capability mapping playbook

Goal:
Create a repeatable playbook and lightweight registry for threading company-level initiatives down to team-level work and mapping them to capabilities, metrics, and dependencies. The intent is to reduce ambiguity when translating strategic pillars into quarterly commitments and to provide a single source of truth for cross-functional trade-offs.

Scope:
- In scope: company-level initiatives, cross-functional dependencies, capability mapping (runtime, console, dedicated, private), decision rubric, rollout guardrails, and a living registry accessible from the roadmap board.
- Out of scope for this ticket: implementation of new metrics collection pipelines (separate ENG project).

Success metrics (what done looks like):
1) A published playbook page and initiative registry table in Confluence with owner, top 3 milestones, expected impact metric, and primary dependencies.
2) Mapping matrix that ties 12 company initiatives to the three strategic pillars and top 5 platform capabilities.
3) < 2 review cycles to reach alignment with Eng, Design, and Sales leadership.
4) Two pilot initiatives threaded across teams during the next planning cycle with documented decisions and dependency resolutions.

Approach and components:
1) Intake rubric: a 6-question form (strategic alignment, customer impact, revenue/ops impact, estimated effort, primary dependencies, launch risk) that yields a 1–9 priority score.
2) Capability map: canonical list of platform capabilities (serving runtime, caching/batching, kernel selection, routing, dedicated autoscale, private deployment, console observability, cost tooling). Each initiative gets 1–3 capability tags.
3) Initiative registry: a single table (Confluence + CSV export) with columns: Initiative key, owner, strategic pillar, capability tags, top metrics, top dependencies (keys), risk level, rollout windows, and notes.
4) Decision rubric: clear thresholds for canary vs. full rollout, required contract with Sales for customer-impacting changes, and fallback model policy for runtime regressions.
5) Governance: monthly cross-functional sync (PM/ENG/Design/Support) for initiatives flagged as high risk or high dependency.

Trade-offs considered:
- Lightweight registry vs. heavy process: we prioritize discoverability and speed; intake form must be quick (<10 mins) to avoid bureaucratic slowdown.
- Centralized ownership vs. team autonomy: playbook enforces a small set of mandatory fields but leaves execution details to team owners.
- Manual tagging vs. automated detection: start manual to capture context, add automation in later iteration when patterns stabilize.

Rollout plan:
- Week 0–2: Draft intake rubric and registry schema; validate with Eng leads and Solutions.
- Week 3: Publish Confluence playbook; create roadmap board view and labels in Linear.
- Week 4–8: Pilot on two initiatives (one runtime-focused, one console/product-focused). Capture lessons and refine.
- Week 9: Finalize playbook and add to onboarding docs for PMs and EMs.

Owner responsibilities:
- PM owner: ensure initiative entry is complete, maintain milestone updates, escalate dependencies.
- ENG owner: provide dependency windows and highlight capacity constraints early.
- Design owner: confirm UX milestones and sign off on any console changes.

Tooling and artifacts:
- Confluence playbook page (canonical source).
- CSV export of registry for program managers and reporting.
- Linear labels: roadmap:initiative, roadmap:pillar-(x), roadmap:capability-(y).
- Templates: intake form, milestone checklist, risk assessment checklist.
