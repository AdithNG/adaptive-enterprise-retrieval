Intermittent 429s from signed-cookie refresh loop in ephemeral worker pool during bulk ingest

Issue summary:
Customer reports repeated 429 responses during a nightly bulk ingest run that stages objects via signed cookies. Failures started after a safe restart of their ingest fleet and coincide with a scheduled canary of a proxy-side keepalive change. BrightFleet uses ephemeral worker pools that refresh signed cookies frequently (~every 90s).

Impact:
- ~20% of ingest batch requests receiving 429s intermittently across a 2 hour window (2026-03-09 03:00–05:00 UTC).
- Customer sees backlog and retry storms causing delayed downstream processing and SLA risk for enterprise tier.

Environment:
- Customer tier: enterprise, VPC peering to prod us-west-2, using pooled edge proxy IPs and signed-cookie approach for pre-signed uploads.
- Ingest orchestrator uses 200 concurrent ephemeral workers per region; each worker refreshes cookies from control-plane every ~90s.

Steps to reproduce (reported by customer):
1) Start parallel uploader with 200 workers using signed-cookie refresh schedule identical to production.
2) Observe steady-state for 10 minutes, then trigger a restart of a subset of workers (simulates canary).
3) Measure HTTP 429s from /upload endpoint and ambiguous throttle headers.

Observed logs / telemetry (customer-provided):
- Repeated 429s with X-RateLimit-Remaining: 0 and X-RateLimit-Reset drifting between 10–60s.
- Ingest orchestrator logs show bursts of signed-cookie refresh requests to the auth service clustered at restart time.
- Edge proxy traces show keepalive connections sticky to pooled IPs; some cookie refresh POSTs get routed through a small subset of origin instances.

Hypothesis / initial triage notes (Support):
- Likely interaction between signed-cookie refresh fanout and token-bucket accounting at the route layer. Ephemeral workers renewing cookies simultaneously can concentrate accounting at the ingress proxy and momentarily exhaust per-route refill credits.
- Proxy keepalive change may have reduced upstream affinity churn, inadvertently concentrating refresh requests to fewer rate-limiter partitions.

Immediate mitigation requested:
- BrightFleet asked for guidance to avoid enqueueing large simultaneous cookie refreshes and whether a server-side smoothing/fanout backoff is possible.

Requested action items:
- SRE: check proxy partitioning/affinity metrics around 2026-03-09 03:00 UTC and correlate with rate-limiter partitions and refill windows.
- Auth team: inspect signed-cookie issuance logs to confirm refresh clustering and whether refresh endpoints are rebalanced across origins.
- Support: provide customer with recommended client-side jitter/backoff pattern and short-term bypass options (e.g., stagger refreshes, use slightly longer cookie TTL while testing).

Notes on customer posture: BrightFleet is sensitive to any required code changes but can deploy a staggered refresh patch to their ingest agents if recommended by Support.

Expected next updates: SRE to post partition metrics and a short tracer link; Support to propose a safe stagger pattern and verify whether an infra-side fast fix (refill smoothing) can be staged for canary revert.
