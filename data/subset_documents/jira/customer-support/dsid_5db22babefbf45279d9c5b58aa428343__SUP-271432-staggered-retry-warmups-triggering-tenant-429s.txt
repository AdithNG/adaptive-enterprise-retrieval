Staggered retry/warmup patterns from client fleet causing tenant-level 429 throttles

Issue summary:
Customer reports recurring tenant-level 429 responses during large fleet rollouts and worker restarts. Behavior appears concentrated during a ~2-6 minute window after a portion of their uploader fleet resumes activity (we see synchronized warmups).

Impact:
- NimbleDocs ingestion pipelines observe 20-40% increased 429s for ~3 minutes after batches of uploader processes restart (~100s of clients).
- Processing latency and backlog grow; customer sees failed items and manual retries.
- Affects production workload (dedicated pool, us-west-2).

Environment:
- Dedicated pool (customer-dedicated-42), prod us-west-2.
- Client SDK: nimble-uploader v1.3.2 (homegrown wrapper that performs exponential backoff with jitter).
- Observed on model routes: /v1/generate and /v1/embeddings (mixed).

Steps to reproduce (from customer repro script):
1) Stop 250 uploader workers (simulate rolling deploy).
2) Bring them back up in groups of ~25 with 10s intervals using their orchestration tool.
3) Each worker immediately resumes sending batched requests (~50 concurrent requests each).
4) After ~30-90s the gateway begins returning 429s for the tenant.

Observed behavior:
- 429 rate spikes for tenant key across both generation and embedding routes; per-key token counts indicate burst window exhaustion.
- Rate limiter logs show many short-lived connections with similar timestamps (client-side jitter clustered bursts).
- Server-side token-bucket windows refilled at fixed intervals causing alignment with client bursts.

Expected behavior:
- Smooth admission across restart/warmup period; backoff/jitter should avoid synchronized bursts that exhaust tenant quota.

Initial hypothesis:
- Clients' backoff/jitter algorithm (uniform jitter applied to entire batch) plus coordinated rolling restarts align retries into micro-bursts.
- Tenant-level burst allowance and quota windowing on the rate-limiter are too tight for the customer's dedicated commit pattern, causing transient 429s even though sustained QPS is within contracted capacity.
- Per-route quotas and token-bucket refills are contributing to momentary alignment effects.

Findings:
- Token-bucket refill granularity aligned with minute boundary (refill event at 00s) which caused many client requests to arrive right after refill resulting in burst depletion.
- Client-side jitter implementation used per-batch seeding leading to near-simultaneous retries across many workers.
- Dedicated pool quota calculations assume smoothing over longer horizons; short transient bursts exceed burst tokens causing 429s even though 5-min average was within contract.
Telemetry checked:
- Gateway logs: connection timestamps clustered within 10-30s windows across restarted workers.
- Rate-limiter metrics: bucket depth dropped to zero for ~120s during incidents.
- No evidence of serving-runtime OOM or GPU shortage; CPU and GPU utilization remained nominal.
Hypothesis validated by staging repro where adding random per-process startup offsets removed the 429 spike.
Short-term mitigation applied (feature-flagged): increased per-tenant burst tokens from 50 -> 200 and modified refill to smooth over two sub-windows to reduce alignment.
Workarounds provided to customer:
- Stagger worker start with randomized delay (recommended 60-180s spread for 250 workers).
- Update uploader SDK to apply jitter per request rather than per batch and add a low-rate warmup ramp (start at 10% of steady concurrency and ramp over 2-5 minutes).
Long-term actions / follow-ups:
- (ENG) Add a tenant-specific smoothing policy in rate-limiter configurable from Console (ENG-4812).
